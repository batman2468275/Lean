# MagSafe add-on: build spec and test plan

The add-on is **passive magnets only**. It holds a MagSafe iPhone against the stand. It does **not** charge it (charging stays on the cable, through the base of the With cutout model). Use "MagSafe-compatible" wording, say nothing that suggests Apple endorses Lean, and use no Apple logos. Suggested fine print: "MagSafe is a trademark of Apple. Lean is not made, approved or endorsed by Apple."

Background, and why it is an arc and not a full ring: `docs/magsafe-feasibility.md`.

## Files

Six print files, one per phone size and model, plus the two plain ones. Generated by `npm run prep:magsafe` (`tools/make_magsafe.py`, inputs in `tools/magsafe_params.json`). Positions for the website are written to `build/magsafe.json`.

| Phone size | Regular | With cutout |
|---|---|---|
| Small | `models/lean-regular-magsafe-small.stl` | `models/lean-cutout-magsafe-small.stl` |
| Medium | `models/lean-regular-magsafe-medium.stl` | `models/lean-cutout-magsafe-medium.stl` |
| Large | `models/lean-regular-magsafe-large.stl` | `models/lean-cutout-magsafe-large.stl` |

Phone sizes: **Small** = iPhone 12 mini and 13 mini (131.5 mm tall). **Medium** = the 6.1 inch iPhone 12, 13, 14, 15 and Pro models (about 147 mm). **Large** = the 6.7 inch Pro Max and Plus models (about 160 mm).

## Magnet spec (per stand)

| | |
|---|---|
| Grade | N52 neodymium, axially magnetised (poles on the flat faces) |
| Size | **6 mm diameter x 2 mm thick** (tolerance +/-0.1 mm; ask the supplier for it in writing) |
| Count | **5** per stand, one behind each of the five central ribs |
| Coating | Nickel-copper-nickel |
| Polarity | Start with **alternating N, S, N, S, N facing the phone**; test against all five facing the same way (see tests) |
| Temperature | N52 starts to lose strength above about 80 C. Do not leave the stand in a hot car or in direct summer sun behind glass |

Why 6 mm and not 8 mm: the magnets sit one per rib, about 8.5 mm apart. With 8 mm magnets the wall between neighbouring pockets came out at 0.16 mm, which cannot be printed. 6 mm leaves a 2.16 mm wall.

## Where the magnets go

The phone's MagSafe ring is a circle about 54 mm across. Its lower arc is what lands on the slope face. The five magnets sit on a 25 mm radius arc (the middle of the ring's 46 to 54 mm band) at x = 0, +/-8.34 and +/-16.66 mm across the stand (the five central rib crests). The height up the slope depends on the phone size (all in mm):

| Size | Assumed ring centre above the phone's bottom edge | Centre magnet, up the slope | Next pair | Outer pair | Free zones for the logo (below / above the magnets) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 60.5 | 36.0 | 37.4 | 42.4 | 6.0 to 27.8 / 50.6 to 66.5 |
| Medium | 67.7 | 43.2 | 44.6 | 49.6 | 6.0 to 35.0 / 57.8 to 66.5 |
| Large | 73.8 | 49.3 | 50.7 | 55.7 | 6.0 to 41.1 / 63.9 to 66.5 |

"Up the slope" is measured from the trough corner where the lip meets the slope. The straight face is 68.5 mm long. The phone's bottom edge is assumed to sit 0.5 mm up the slope (as in the 3D scene; the brief says "about 5 mm", which would move every magnet 4.5 mm up).

**The ring height is an assumption.** I set it to 0.46 x the phone's height (60.5, 67.7 and 73.8 mm), the middle of the brief's 60 to 75 mm range for a 147 mm phone. Nothing here has been checked against a real phone. Measure from the bottom edge of a real phone to the centre of its MagSafe ring (a MagSafe charger or a stick-on ring helps you find the centre), put the figure in `tools/magsafe_params.json` as `ring_centre_mm` for that size, and run `npm run prep:magsafe`.

## Pocket and pad dimensions

The slope is ribbed (crest to valley 2.24 mm, ribs 8.33 mm apart) and the phone only touches the crests. A pocket cut straight into the ribs would break through the valleys, and sinking the magnet below the valleys would put it 3 mm or more from the phone, which kills most of the pull. So each magnet gets a **flat pad** that fills the valleys up to the crest plane, and the pocket is cut into the pad from the front.

| Feature | Dimension |
|---|---|
| Pad top | flat, 0.02 mm above the rib crest plane (so the phone rests on the pads) |
| Pad size | 8.8 mm across at the top, flaring to 12.4 mm over 3.5 mm depth, blending into the ribs |
| Pocket diameter | **6.3 mm** (6 mm magnet + 0.15 mm clearance all round) |
| Pocket depth | **2.2 mm** (2.0 mm magnet, so it sits 0.2 mm below the pad surface) |
| Entry chamfer | 0.4 mm |
| Plastic behind the magnet | **7.8 mm** at most positions (up to 19 mm near the top of the face). The brief's 0.6 to 1.0 mm floor is a minimum; the slope plate here is about 10 mm thick |
| Wall between neighbouring pockets | at least 2.16 mm |
| Distance to the lip and cable slot | at least 5 mm (the slot is at the base; the pads start about 30 mm up) |

See `docs/magsafe-geometry-check.png` (height map and a cross-section through the centre pocket of the Large With cutout file).

## Assembly

1. Choose a print orientation so the pockets print cleanly (a pocket facing straight down will sag). Check your slicer preview.
2. Test-fit one magnet in each pocket. FDM holes often print slightly small: if a magnet does not drop in with light finger pressure, ream the pocket gently by hand with a 6.3 mm drill bit, or change `clearance_radial` and regenerate.
3. Identify the pole of each magnet before it goes in (use a magnet of known polarity). In the alternating pattern neighbouring magnets attract each other strongly: insert them one at a time with plastic tweezers.
4. Put a small drop of cyanoacrylate or two-part epoxy in each pocket, seat the magnet flat (it should sit 0.2 mm below the pad) and let it cure before handling.
5. Check each magnet's facing pole against the plan before the glue cures. A magnet glued the wrong way round cannot be fixed without removing it.

## What must be test-printed and tested before we sell this

I cannot verify any of this from code. No pull force has been measured, and none is claimed.

1. **Print a coupon first**: one pad with pockets at 6.2, 6.3 and 6.4 mm on your printer and filament, to find the diameter that fits your magnets.
2. **Measure a real phone**: ring centre height for each size (see above). If it differs from the assumed value by more than about 2 mm, regenerate and reprint, because the self-alignment budget of a MagSafe ring is only about 1.5 mm (secondary source).
3. **Hold test with a real phone for each size**, in the stand: does it stay put when you tap the screen, swipe, plug a cable in and out, and nudge the stand? Compare with the same phone in a stand without magnets.
4. **Polarity test**: alternating N, S, N, S, N against all five facing the same way. Keep the better one. If neither holds noticeably better than no magnets, do not sell the add-on.
5. **Case test**: bare phone, a thin MagSafe case, a thick case, and a non-MagSafe case. Write the result into the product copy.
6. **Seating**: does the phone sit properly on the pads (no rocking, no gap at the lip)?
7. **Glue and heat**: does each magnet stay put after a few hundred times of putting the phone on and off, and after a warm day?
8. **Safety and rules**: five small strong magnets are a swallowing hazard. Check the magnet and small-parts rules for each market you ship to, add a clear warning on the page and the packaging, and note that strong magnets should be kept away from pacemakers.
9. **Cost**: five N52 magnets plus assembly time against a $1.50 add-on. Get a quote before launching.

## Limits to state honestly on the page

- It holds the phone; it does not charge it.
- Pick the size of your phone. A phone of another size will not line up and the hold will be weak.
- Thick cases weaken the hold.
- It is a partial MagSafe hold (five magnets on the lower arc), not a full MagSafe ring, and it will not self-centre like a MagSafe charger.
